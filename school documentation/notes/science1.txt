## **Modern Biotechnology & Genetic Engineering**

### **Core Definition**

* **Modern Biotechnology:** The manipulation of genetic material (DNA, genes, cells, and microorganisms) using advanced techniques to alter an organism's traits, commonly referred to as genetic engineering.



### **Key Techniques & Processes**

* **DNA Fingerprinting:**
* Used to identify individuals based on their unique DNA sequences.


* While organisms of the same species share similar general sequences, individual uniqueness is determined by **polymorphic markers**.


* Common biological samples collected for testing include blood, saliva, urine, hair follicles, and semen.




* **Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR):** Also known as **molecular photocopying**, a laboratory technique used to generate millions or billions of copies of a specific DNA segment.


* **Gene Therapy:** A treatment method for genetic diseases designed to avoid major medical procedures or surgery by inserting genetic material into a patient's cells.


* **In Vivo Gene Therapy:** Genetic material is packaged and injected *directly* into the patient's body without removing cells.


* **Ex Vivo Gene Therapy:** Cells are removed from the patient's body, altered in a laboratory setting, and then returned.





### **Reproductive Technologies**

* **In Vitro Fertilization (IVF):** A technique where an egg cell and a sperm cell are fertilized outside the body in a controlled laboratory Petri dish.


* The fertilized egg becomes a **zygote**, which develops into an **embryo**.


* The embryo is subsequently transferred and implanted into the female's uterus.




* **Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection (ICSI):** A specialized form of IVF where a single sperm cell is injected directly into an egg cell.

## **Modern Biotechnology & Genetic Engineering**

### **Primary Concepts & Goals**

* **Modern Biotechnology Goals:** The direct manipulation and alteration of genetic material (DNA, cells, organisms) in a laboratory setting to solve biological problems (such as diseases caused by viruses or bacteria) and produce desired traits.
* **Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs / GMO Products):** Organisms whose DNA has been altered in a laboratory using genetic engineering techniques to enhance existing traits or introduce new ones (e.g., increased nutritional value, pest resistance, herbicide tolerance).

### **Local GMO Crops in the Philippines**

* In 2003, the Philippines became the first country in its region to commercialize and cultivate biotech crops, modifying key staples including corn, eggplant, cotton, and rice.
* **Cry1Ac Gene:** A gene derived from the soil bacterium *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) that is inserted into plants to enable toxin production, effectively deterring specific destructive pests.
* **Bt Crops:** Bt corn, Bt eggplant, and Bt cotton are genetically modified to protect against pests like the corn borer and the eggplant fruit and shoot borer (EFSB), thereby safeguarding crop quality and yield.
* **Golden Rice (Malusog Rice):** Genetically enhanced rice containing inserted genes from corn and bacteria to synthesize beta-carotene (a precursor to Vitamin A). Its main objectives are to:
1. Combat Vitamin A deficiency.
2. Reduce childhood mortality.
3. Prevent blindness.
4. Strengthen overall immune systems.



### **Gene Therapy & Delivery Methods**

* **Gene Therapy:** A genetic treatment method for hereditary or genetic diseases (e.g., hemophilia, cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anemia) that aims to modify or correct a patient's faulty genes rather than relying solely on traditional medical procedures or surgeries.
* **In Vivo Gene Therapy:** The direct injection or delivery of therapeutic genetic material into the patient's body to target specific cells internally.
* **Ex Vivo Gene Therapy:** A process where target cells are extracted from the patient, genetically altered in a laboratory environment, and then reinfused or reinserted back into the patient's body.

## **Key Academic Terms Glossary**

* **Genetically Modified Organism (GMO):** An organism whose genetic material has been altered using genetic engineering technology.
* **Cry1Ac Gene:** The specific bacterial gene responsible for producing insecticidal proteins in Bt crops.
* **Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt):** A soil-dwelling bacterium used as a natural source for insect-resistant genes.
* **Bt Corn / Bt Eggplant / Bt Cotton:** Crops genetically engineered with bacterial genes to resist destructive agricultural pests.
* **Eggplant Fruit and Shoot Borer (EFSB):** A major pest targeted by genetic modification in eggplant varieties.
* **Golden Rice / Malusog Rice:** Biofortified rice engineered to produce beta-carotene.
* **Beta-carotene / Vitamin A Precursor:** A biological compound converted by the body into Vitamin A.
* **Gene Therapy:** Medical intervention that alters gene expression to treat or cure genetic disorders.
* **In Vivo Gene Therapy:** Direct in-body gene delivery.
* **Ex Vivo Gene Therapy:** Laboratory-based genetic modification of extracted cells prior to reintroduction.
* **Hemophilia:** A hereditary genetic disorder impairing the body's ability to control blood clotting.
* **Cystic Fibrosis:** A progressive, genetic disease causing persistent lung damage and restricting breathing passageways.
* **Sickle Cell Anemia:** A group of inherited red blood cell disorders characterized by misshapen cells that restrict healthy blood flow.



### **Local Applications & Examples in the Philippines**

* Mentioned Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs) developed or utilized in the country include corn, eggplants (*talong*), and rice.
